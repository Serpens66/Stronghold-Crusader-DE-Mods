# Remapped-player Lord spawn audit

Audited native build: `FBCB93195FC7EFCA9BDAC5204852EFDD76F9818F59A6711750D77C9CEF2831E2`.

The custom-game initialization path at RVA `0x94350` derives the map-position slot for each occupied lobby player through RVA `0x89870`. It then calls RVA `0xCFE00` with the old map slot and new player slot. This wrapper applies the reassignment to units, player resources, and related managers.

RVA `0x89870` searches an eight-entry signed-byte permutation and returns the one-based source position whose stored value matches the requested final player ID. The occupied-player remap loop at `0x94350` accepts either side of the human/AI setup representation before the later initialization code branches between those representations. The remap itself is therefore not AI-specific. Managed multiplayer subsequently applies the same native `loadPlayerRemapping` result to ordinary human `MPGameMember.playerID` records as well as creating the separate AI records.

RVA `0x198FB0`, called first by `0xCFE00`, scans live units. Ordinary units owned by either reassigned slot receive the other owner ID. Unit types `0x37`, `0x38`, and `0x39` take a separate branch and are instead assigned unit state `0x6E`; they are not transferred to the new player ID.

RVA `0xC6810` then swaps a fixed list of four-byte fields between the two `GamePlayerResources` records when a live building was reassigned. The list includes the Keep and its associated start-position resources. It does not include `r_LordUnitId` at record offset `0x21F8` or `r_LordUnitGlobalId` at `0x21FC`.

The periodic player initializer at RVA `0xCEF00` invokes RVA `0xC23C0` for player IDs 1 through 8 when its resource gates pass. In `0xC23C0`, the Lord field at `r_LordUnitId` is tested before any identity validation. A nonzero value bypasses the type-`0x37` spawn call at RVA `0x17FEF0`. The stored global ID is checked only on the later existing-Lord path, so a stale nonzero unit ID can suppress creation indefinitely.

When the field is zero, `0xC23C0` calls `0x17FEF0` with one-based owner/player IDs, world coordinates derived from the reassigned Keep position, terrain height, and unit type `0x37`. A successful return is written to `r_LordUnitId`; the unit's global ID is then written to `r_LordUnitGlobalId`, chores are reset at RVA `0x196F80`, and the unit is advanced from `NeedsInit` to the active Lord path. This is the authoritative Vanilla creation sequence.

Runtime evidence from `The Ford Across the River` with synchronized roster players 1 and 8 showed player 8 owning the valid reassigned Keep while already carrying `r_LordUnitId=8` and `r_LordUnitGlobalId=534` on the first simulation tick. That reference failed the installed Script Extender's unit identity validation and remained unchanged through the Vanilla Lord window. Player 1 began with `0/0` and received a valid Lord at the normal counter boundary. Both players had `WinLossState.None`; defeat state was not causal.

The map-specific contrast is visible in repeated tick-1 captures, but later two-player-map tests disprove cross-slot reassignment as a sufficient trigger. On the two-position `The Ford Across the River`, final players 7 and 8 owned the two reassigned Keeps (building IDs `1` and `10`) while retaining tombstones `4/517` and `8/534`. Four other two-position maps performed the same effective low-slot reassignment without the defect: `Bow Ridge`, `Inches Apart`, and `Its just not fair` assigned Keeps `1` and `10`, while `Lionheart` assigned Keeps `1` and `12`; in all four cases both final players' Lord identities were `0/0` during ticks 1 through 3 and Vanilla created valid Lords at the normal initialization boundary. On the tested eight-position `Crater Lake`, players 7 and 8 owned their native high-slot Keeps (building IDs `859` and `949`) and likewise began at `0/0`.

Direct decompression of the installed map files establishes the upstream origin. Current DE maps store the nine 22,588-byte player records in section `2022` and the 10,000 1,168-byte unit records in section `3015` (rather than the historical HD section IDs `1022` and `1015`). `The Ford Across the River` itself serializes nonzero Lord identities in every unused player record 3 through 8. Records 7 and 8 contain exactly `4/517` and `8/534`; both referenced unit slots are entirely zero, and no unit in section `3015` has global ID `517` or `534`. Only records 1 and 2 have corresponding serialized Lord units. In contrast, `Bow Ridge`, `Inches Apart`, `Its just not fair`, and `Lionheart` serialize `0/0` for records 7 and 8.

The necessary failing combination is therefore narrower and data-driven: Ford carries stale Lord identities in otherwise unused destination player records, then the player-slot remap transfers Keep/start fields into those records while `0xC6810` leaves their Lord fields untouched. Maps entering the same remap with zero Lord identities in the destination records are unaffected. `0xC23C0` subsequently trusts Ford's stale nonzero ID and skips creation even though its unit slot is empty. The bug is thus triggered by malformed/stale built-in map state plus Vanilla's incomplete remap sanitation, not by Ford geometry or two-player remapping alone.

Subsequent mode-independent runtime validation reproduced the same exact player-8 tombstone (`8/534`, owner `0`, `CHIMP_TYPE_NULL`, `AliveState.None`, unit global ID `0`, health `0`) when the map was launched as a `CustomTrail`. The guarded clear ran once and Vanilla created the correctly owned replacement Lord as unit ID `12` with global ID `5970`. This confirms that the faulty state is not confined to APIShared's `CustomGame` classification; custom-trail startup reaches the same underlying remap behavior.

The safe managed workaround is therefore limited to the first three simulation ticks of a new gameplay session, independent of its managed game-mode classification: scan the native one-based player records 1 through 8 and require `WinLossState.None`, a live owned Keep, and a live owned Keep-door/start-marker reference. A synchronized `gameMembers` roster, when present, contributes only a kicked-player veto; roster absence or membership is not an allowlist because that managed collection is launch-path-dependent. Clear both Lord identity fields through the public `GamePlayerManagerAPI` setters only when the stored unit/global IDs are both nonzero and the referenced unit slot has the exact observed tombstone signature: owner `0`, `CHIMP_TYPE_NULL`, `AliveState.None`, global ID `0`, and current health `0`. A missing or otherwise invalid unit is not sufficient. Vanilla then remains responsible for creation. Loaded saves remain excluded because their restored identities are authoritative and no subsequent new-game Lord spawn is guaranteed; editor sessions have no corresponding gameplay spawn contract. All non-tombstone identities, zero stored identities, defeated players, and later runtime Lord deaths remain untouched. The native defect is proven on the underlying custom-game remap path, and runtime evidence now confirms that `CustomTrail` can reach the same faulty state; the state-based guard deliberately avoids treating either managed mode label as an eligibility requirement.

Confidence is structural for `0xC6810` field coverage and `0xC23C0` control flow, and candidate for the broader roles of `0x94350`, `0x89870`, `0xCFE00`, and `0x198FB0`. The second diagnostic build confirmed the complete tombstone signature for both remapped players 7 and 8 before clearing their stale references, then observed Vanilla create correctly owned Lords with matching nonzero global IDs.

Human-player exposure and deterministic peer behavior are runtime-confirmed. In a two-peer `The Ford Across the River` launch, the host was player 1 and the client's own per-player settings advertisement reported player ID 8 after the synchronized lobby roster converged to `[1,8]`. On simulation tick 1, both peers independently observed player 8 owning Keep `10` and door/start marker `14` with the exact `8/534` zeroed-unit tombstone, then cleared it through the public setters. Both peers observed Vanilla create unit ID `5`, global ID `5886`, owner `8` at tick 96 and the Lord become alive at tick 97; both emitted the same confirmation and neither log contained a desync, CRC, or simulation-mismatch indicator before the session ended. The defect and the state-based correction are therefore not AI-specific, and the correction decision plus resulting Lord identity were identical on host and client.
